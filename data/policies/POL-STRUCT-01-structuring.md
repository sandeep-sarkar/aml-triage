# POL-STRUCT-01: Structuring and threshold avoidance

## Summary

Structuring (also known as "smurfing") is the practice of breaking a large
cash transaction into a series of smaller transactions, each kept under a
reporting threshold, in order to avoid triggering mandatory reporting.

## Indicators

- Multiple cash deposits or transfers, each below the £10,000 reporting
  threshold, occurring within a short window (typically under 72 hours).
- Deposits made just under the threshold with no clear business rationale
  (e.g. £8,500-£9,900 rather than round figures).
- Use of multiple channels or branches to spread transactions.
- Pattern is inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation, business
  activity, or historical transaction behaviour.

## Required action

Where three or more transactions in a rolling 72-hour window meet the
indicators above, the alert must be escalated to a Level 2 investigator.
The disposition must cite this policy and summarise the specific
transactions and dates that triggered escalation.

## Non-triggers

A pattern of transactions under the threshold is not itself sufficient to
escalate if:
- The customer has a documented, ongoing business reason for frequent
  sub-threshold cash handling (e.g. a retail business with daily takings),
  and the pattern is consistent with prior KYC-documented activity.
- The transactions are spread over more than 7 days with no clustering.
